## 2. Measurement — what is actually true

Everything here was measured in this environment before any code was written.

### 2.1 The genre is large and its epigraph is perfectly uniform

127 of 233 documents (**54.5%**): 109 `sc_cosit`, 12 `sci_cosit`, 4 `sd_cosit`,
2 bare `sc`. Every one opens with a first non-empty paragraph of the shape:

```
Solução de Consulta Cosit nº 100, de 28 de setembro de 2020
Solução de Consulta Interna Cosit nº 10, de 5 de junho de 2014
Solução de Divergência Cosit nº 10, de 14 de agosto de 2014
Solução de Consulta Disit/SRRF03 nº 15, de 9 de março de 2009
Solução de Divergência COSIT Nº 16 DE 27/09/2012          ← the one variant shape
```

So an epigraph pattern is reliable at 0.9 for the whole genre — no fallback
guessing needed.

### 2.2 The profile gap is currently filled by *three wrong answers*

Measured over all 233 with `select_profile`:

| Profile selected | `sc_cosit` | `sci_cosit` | `sc` | `sd_cosit` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `generic` | 97 | 12 | 2 | 4 |
| `jurisprudencia_generico` | **10** | — | — | — |
| `servico` | **2** | — | — | — |

The 10 + 2 are a **defect the plan does not name**, and they are the exact bug
shape Cycle 2 fixed as m-6 on `servico`: an *unanchored* authority pattern.
`jurisprudencia_generico.authority_res` carries a bare
`superior\s+tribunal\s+de\s+justica`, which matches any document that merely
*mentions* the STJ — and a solução de consulta discussing STJ case law does.
They score 0.40 on that alone, with no epigraph match.

The consequence is not only routing. Two of them acquire a **wrong URN type**:

```
sc_cosit_116_20190326   urn:lex:br:…:servico:2019-03-26;116     ← should be solucao.consulta
sc_cosit_14_20170116    urn:lex:br:…:servico:2017-01-16;14      ← should be solucao.consulta
```

### 2.3 Finding A's real cause — and why the plan's expectation is wrong

**125 of the 127** carry all three bare headings `Relatório`, `Fundamentos`,
`Conclusão` as standalone paragraphs. The plan calls this a "stereotyped
skeleton … the generic recogniser is not looking for", and that is right. But
the plan's implied remedy — that this is a recogniser gap the referee covers —
is **not** what the code does. Measured:

```
sc_cosit_100  idx=22  'Relatório'    upper_ratio=0.11  prose_form=False  style='Normal'
sc_cosit_100  idx=42  'Fundamentos'  upper_ratio=0.09  prose_form=False  style='Normal'
sc_cosit_100  idx=164 'Conclusão'    upper_ratio=0.11  prose_form=False  style='Normal'
```

These headings are **title-case**. `is_prose_form_header` requires
`upper_ratio >= PROSE_HEADER_MIN_UPPER` (0.85); they score ~0.10. So they are
never even *proposed*, the referee is never asked about them, and no amount of
referee budget can recover them. `par_cosit_26`'s `RELATÓRIO` — the paragraph
A-H.1 was written for — is upper-case, which is why that amendment worked and
why this case was never noticed.

**This is why profile data alone cannot discharge deliverable 2**, and it is the
cycle's central design finding.

### 2.4 The flatness baseline, and a discrepancy with the plan

Measured rules-only (`--referee=none`, which is what the suite pins per plan
§9.3):

| Scope | Flat | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Whole corpus | **115** | 233 |
| `sc_cosit` | **58** | 109 |
| `sci_cosit` | 8 | 12 |
| `sd_cosit` | 3 | 4 |
| `sc` | 2 | 2 |
| `ad_pgfn` | 14 | 15 |

The plan's §1.1 reports 107/233 and 52 flat `sc_cosit`. That run was
**refereed** (DeepSeek + cache); §1.8 records the referee moving flat 115 → 107.
The two numbers are consistent — they measure different configurations. Q-2
settles which one this cycle's headline is measured against.

### 2.5 Authority and labels are already nearly right

Across the 127: 113 resolve authority from the epigraph, 9 from the preamble
(the STJ false-positive group), 2+2+1 take a default. Front-matter labels are
dominated by `ASSUNTO` (306) and `DISPOSITIVOS LEGAIS` (242), then `EMENTA` (25).

### 2.6 No sample is touched

A `solução de consulta|divergência` epigraph pattern matches **none** of the 15
samples' first 30 paragraphs. Verified directly against the styled goldens. So
the new profile cannot claim a sample, and the "no golden moves" exit criterion
starts from a sound position.

## 3. Reconciliation — three binding answers

Asked before implementation, per the skill's "ask rather than assume" rule.

### Q-1 — How should the title-case headings be admitted? → **Profile-declared section headings**

Given §2.3, the options were: a profile-declared heading list; relaxing
`PROSE_HEADER_MIN_UPPER` globally; profile-declared but still referee-confirmed;
or measure-only.

**Answer: profile-declared section headings, admitted deterministically.**
`DocumentProfile` gains a `section_res` field — literal, per-genre heading
patterns. A match admits the paragraph as a section with no referee.

The reasoning that makes this safe rather than a widening of invariant #8: the
patterns are **genre knowledge declared as data**, scoped to a profile, and
every existing profile declares none — so no document that does not select
`solucao_consulta` can change behaviour. This is precisely what a profile is
for, and it keeps the rules-only run deterministic and reproducible, which
Q-2 then depends on.

### Q-2 — Which flatness baseline? → **Rules-only, `--referee=none`**

The before/after is measured at 115/233 and `sc_cosit` 58/109. Deterministic,
offline, no API key, and assertable in the suite. The refereed comparison is
Cycle 6's business, where referee economics belong.

### Q-3 — Fix the `jurisprudencia_generico` false positive here? → **Yes, in this cycle**

Anchor its `authority_res` the way Cycle 2 anchored `servico`'s (m-6). Leaving
it would have the new profile fighting a mis-scoring incumbent for its own
genre. Scope is that one profile — **not** a sweep of all profiles.

### Non-blocking decisions taken myself

- The profile is named `solucao_consulta` (the plan's name) and covers all four
  sub-genres, with `urn_type_res` distinguishing them — Cycle 2's A-2.2
  mechanism, reused rather than reinvented.
- It registers **before** `generic` and after `servico`, so `generic` stays the
  floor.
- The parked `generic` siglas are **copied**, not moved, where `generic` still
  needs them for non-`sc` documents; §4.2 states exactly which.
- New tests go in `tests/unit/test_profile_solucao_consulta.py`, with
  corpus-dependent ones behind the `requires_corpus` skip that
  `test_urn_completeness.py` established.

## 4. Expanded goals

### 4.1 `DocumentProfile.section_res` — the new admission route *(major M-1)*

```python
#: Literal section headings this genre always uses ("Relatório", "Fundamentos",
#: "Conclusão"). Matched against the folded, stripped paragraph text.
section_res: tuple[re.Pattern[str], ...] = ()
```

Empty on all six existing profiles ⇒ their behaviour is byte-identical.

### 4.2 `profile/solucao_consulta.py` — the new profile

```python
SOLUCAO_CONSULTA = DocumentProfile(
    name="solucao_consulta",
    urn_type="solucao.consulta",
    urn_type_res=(
        (r"^\s*solucao\s+de\s+consulta\s+interna\b", "solucao.consulta.interna"),
        (r"^\s*solucao\s+de\s+divergencia\b",        "solucao.divergencia"),
        (r"^\s*solucao\s+de\s+consulta\b",           "solucao.consulta"),
    ),
    urn_authority=None,
    epigraph_res=(r"^\s*solucao\s+de\s+consulta\b", r"^\s*solucao\s+de\s+divergencia\b"),
    authority_res=(),            # deliberately empty — see the note below
    authority_map=(COSIT, DISIT, SRRF, RFB, SRF),
    field_labels=frozenset({"ASSUNTO", "EMENTA", "DISPOSITIVOS LEGAIS", …}),
    section_res=(r"^relatorio$", r"^fundamentos?$", r"^conclusao$"),
    ementa_absent=False,
    closing_res=(the shared closing pattern),
)
```

`authority_res` is empty **on purpose**: §2.2 is a cautionary tale about an
unanchored authority pattern, and the epigraph already carries the sigla for
113 of 127. Adding a loose "Receita Federal" pattern here would recreate the
very bug this cycle fixes.

### 4.3 Threading `section_res` into hierarchy *(major M-2)*

`build_tree` gains one keyword-only parameter:

```python
def build_tree(blocks, *, span=None, doc_name="", section_res=(), referee=None, log=None, logger=None)
```

defaulting to `()`. `infer_hierarchy` passes `profile.section_res` — it already
resolves the profile. Every other caller passes nothing and is unaffected.

Inside, declared headings join the existing `prose_form_indices` set, so they
reuse A-H.4's depth logic unchanged — the logic whose own comment reads
"`RELATÓRIO` and `CONCLUSÃO` are not `1.` and `2.` of anything", written for
exactly these headings.

A declared heading is **not** a referee confirmation, so it carries its own
weight and signal:

- `evidence.py`: `W_SECTION_DECLARED = 0.8`
- `unify.py`: signals `("prose_form", "profile_declared")`

Annex spans pass `section_res=()` — an annex is not the document's own skeleton.

### 4.4 `jurisprudencia_generico.authority_res` anchored *(minor)*

Require the court name to open the line or follow a tribunal-naming cue, so a
passing mention in body prose cannot claim the document.

### 4.5 Re-measurement (deliverable 3)

Before/after flat counts, whole corpus and per genre, rules-only, recorded in
the report.

## 8. Risks

1. **Adding a profile changes scoring for every document** — the plan's own
   stated risk. Mitigated by §2.6 (no sample matches) and E-2 (goldens pinned).
   Any movement is escalated, not absorbed.
2. **`section_res` is a new admission route that bypasses the referee.** Bounded
   by being per-profile and empty everywhere else; `test_a_profile_declaring_none_is_unchanged`
   is the guard.
3. **`EXPECTED_REGISTRY` must change** — an existing regression test's data.
   Logged as a change; it is the test's purpose to notice a new profile.
4. **A declared heading inside a quoted passage** would be fabricated structure.
   Guarded by reusing the existing `quoted` check.
