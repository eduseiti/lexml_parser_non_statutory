**Two real defects in this cycle's own code were found by its own tests**, both
silent to every schema and both caught only by reading the output:

1. **Duplication.** The first implementation promoted the norm's name to
   `NomeAgrupador` *and* left it at the head of the paragraph. Caught by a
   `Counter` comparison across emitters.
2. **Loss.** The fix then consumed the separator into the cut, losing two `-`
   and two commas. Caught by tightening that comparison from words to
   characters.

**One real gap in delivered code** was found the same way: `adjudicate` did not
enforce `VOCABULARIES`. The transports each validated the answer they parsed,
but an in-process referee reached `adjudicate` directly — so `AlwaysOwnReferee`
was answering `"own"` to boundary questions and having it recorded as an
override. Now an out-of-vocabulary answer is an abstention.

## 7. Deviations and open items

- **Nothing in the cycle was left undone.**
- The record's §8 counter-argument stands and is *not* settled by this cycle:
  the whole nesting payoff on the visible corpus is 4 child sections in 1 of 15
  samples. Whether multi-norm runs are common in the 300+ unseen documents is
  **Cycle 9's question**, and it now arrives with `runs` in place to measure it.
- **A-Q.7 improves the prompt, not the model.** The record's §2.3 wrong
  overrides were measured against a live provider; the recorded fixtures assert
  plumbing (A-4b.5). Whether the widened context actually changes what a live
  model answers is unverified here, and is the same open question as P-5.
- `--referee=api` now changes emitted XML. Any future live run over the corpus
  should be reviewed with that in mind: it can no longer only move a route.
